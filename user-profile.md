## Core themes

- Structural estimation of games, network formation and dynamic discrete choice (CCP, simulation, moment-based)
- Panel data identification (binary outcomes, fixed effects, recovering interaction matrices from panels)
- Duration / hazard models with interdependence
- Using subjective expectations and elicited beliefs as data
- Measurement error, indirect elicitation (list randomisation), biomarker validation of self-reports
- Natural-experiment designs (volcanic pollution), machine-learning tools (signatures, conformal inference)

## How to write the "connection" line

- **Pick the most concrete overlap.** Don't say "both work on econometrics." Say "Both recover interaction structure from panel outcomes (you: tax-competition networks; them: peer effects in classrooms)."
- **If they're applied,** name the identification or estimation problem in their setting that one of Áureo's tools speaks to (unobserved networks, multiple equilibria, interdependent durations, expectations data).
- **If they're a theorist or econometrician,** connect to a specific open identification/inference question in Áureo's current working papers.
- **If there's no direct overlap,** find a *conversation hook* — a substantive question their work raises that touches one of Áureo's themes obliquely.
- **Always actionable** — write a sentence Áureo could literally say at the start of the meeting.
- **Mind the editor role.** Áureo is a Co-Editor at *Econometrica*; never suggest discussing a paper's submission or refereeing status at a journal Áureo edits.
